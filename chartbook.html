The chartbook

The market in charts: positioning, inventories, prices, cracks, and the Canadian wing, each with its read and its data vintage.

How to read this page: inventory charts print the level against the ten-year seasonal average, in standard deviations and as a percentile of readings since 2010. Positioning charts print net length with its percentile over the history shown. Every chart carries the vintage of its data; nothing here is fresher than its source.

Positioning

Who holds what, from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) Commitments of Traders reports and the Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) equivalent. Net length in contracts; each percentile is against the series' own history.

Noncommercial traders, the speculative category in the legacy Commitments of Traders report, hold a net long of 100,343 contracts in NYMEX WTI futures as of October 13, 2026, in the 4th percentile of weekly readings since 2015. Positioning is the crowdedness gauge for the outright: a stretched net long is flush fuel on any bearish surprise, and a stretched net short is squeeze fuel the other way. Prices and spreads

The outright and the differential, for context: the spreads are the trade, and these are the levels they are struck off.

WTI front month closed at $99.88 on October 22, 2026, in the 94th percentile of daily closes since 2020 and +5.29 dollars over the last thirty days. The outright is context rather than the trade: the differentials and calendar spreads this site follows are struck off this level, and its trend sets the tone they trade in. A sustained break of the recent range, either way, would reset that context.

The assessed WCS discount at Hardisty was $18.57 a barrel in the last published reading, August 31, 2026 (Natural Resources Canada publishes monthly, about two months in arrears), in the 73rd percentile of daily assessments since 2021. Cracks

The refining margin: three barrels of crude into two of gasoline and one of distillate, priced off front-month futures.

3-2-1 crack spread

Computed daily from front-month WTI, RBOB gasoline, and heating oil futures. Data through October 22, 2026.

$52.30crack, latest close
91thpercentile since 2020
$75.31series high, Aug 2026

The 3-2-1 crack spread printed $52.30 a barrel on October 22, 2026, in the 91st percentile of daily readings since 2020. The crack is the refining system's margin: at this level refiners run as hard as equipment allows, which is crude demand support, and product supply eventually answers. A crack back under twenty-five dollars would signal margin-driven run cuts and soften the crude bid.

The Canadian wing

The wing the global chartbooks do not carry: Alberta storage from the Alberta Energy Regulator (AER) ST3 release, exports and egress capacity from Canada Energy Regulator (CER) data, Enbridge Mainline apportionment (history and scoring on the Apportionment page), and positioning in the differential future itself.

Alberta crude inventories stand at 60.0 million barrels, 3.4 million barrels (5.3 percent) below the ten-year seasonal average, a gap of 0.5 standard deviations, and in the 62nd percentile of readings for this part of the year since 2010. Stocks built 1.3 million barrels on the month. This is the basin's buffer, and the earliest physical read on whether egress is keeping up with supply: what cannot leave accumulates here. It is also the print the satellite shadow read on Tank Watch is scored against, two months before ST3 publishes. A draw back to the seasonal average over the next two prints would retire the build story.

Canadian crude exports averaged 4,616 thousand barrels a day in June 2026, the latest Canada Energy Regulator print, against a demonstrated egress capacity stack of 4,984 thousand barrels a day in the site's egress model: roughly 368 thousand barrels a day of headroom. Exports are what the pipes and rails exist to carry. When this line presses the capacity line, apportionment rises and the differential widens; supply growth that closes the gap, or a capacity step that widens it, changes the read.

Enbridge apportionment notices, as pinned in apportionment_seen.json. Latest: November 2026.

7%heavy, Nov 2026
8% / 4%light / heavy, Oct 2026

Enbridge set November 2026 Mainline apportionment at 19 percent for light crude and 7 percent for heavy at Kerrobert, against 8 and 4 percent in October. Apportionment is the basin's congestion price in percentage form: it is the share of nominated barrels the pipe cannot take, and it leads the differential when it steps. The full history and the prediction scoring record are on the Apportionment page. A return to zero would say the pressure has cleared; another step up would say the squeeze is still building.

Sources & notes

  1. Method: seasonal averages and standard deviations are computed over the prior ten years for the same week (or month) of the year, plus or minus one; percentiles are against readings since 2010, or since the start of the series where the series is younger, as printed on each chart. The grammar follows the conventions of the published energy chartbook literature; every chart is built from primary public data by this site's own code, and the words are ours. ↩
  2. The physically delivered NYMEX WTI contract appears only in the legacy Commitments of Traders report, whose noncommercial category is the speculative proxy used on the WTI positioning chart. Brent and the TMW diff future use the disaggregated and ICE reports' managed-money category. ↩
  3. The satellite tank-level shadow read (Tank Watch) is deliberately not charted here: it stays in shadow mode until it earns promotion against the ST3 print. Alberta inventories above are the official ST3 series. ↩
