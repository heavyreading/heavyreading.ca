NOTES

Disambiguation, in one line: Inter Pipeline's Milk River system since 2021 and the CER Milk River trunkline in the egress stack are the same pipeline. There is no second Milk River pipeline and no Milk River gathering system in WS-H; the gathering upstream of the interconnect is Bow River.

Do not carry Bow River numbers across the interconnect: the 180,000 b/d ultimate, the 85,300 b/d 2020 throughput and the 32,500 b/d take-or-pay all belong to Bow River. Milk River's own figures are 97.9 thousand b/d capacity (CER) and approx 90,000 b/d throughput in 2021.

Pre-2021 sources show this pipeline as Plains. Post-2021 sources that still say Plains are stale, including any tariff summary that was never updated after the swap.

Which system this is, stated plainly: this sheet is the Milk River Pipeline, legal name Milk River Pipeline Ltd., operated by Inter Pipeline Ltd. since June 1, 2021: a short CER-regulated trunkline (two approx 16 km pipelines) from the Bow River interconnect near Milk River, Alberta to the Front Range interconnect at the Canada-U.S. border west of Coutts. In the Heavy Reading balance model the same steel is the egress-stack line with system key milk_river (nameplate 98 kb/d). WS-H name and model key are two names for one pipeline, not two pipelines.

Which system this is not: it is not a gathering system. The Milk River Pipeline gathers nothing at battery or field grain; its only receipt point is the Bow River interconnect (CER). The southern Alberta gathering that feeds it, and the segregated Hardisty to Milk River transmission leg, are the Bow River Pipeline System, a separate Inter Pipeline system, drawn on this map in a separate line and under its own WS-H system name so the hand-off at the interconnect reads on the page. Same owner, different system: owner sameness is the trap this map exists to disarm.

Not a Plains system any more: Milk River was Plains Midstream Canada until the acquisition closed June 1, 2021 (WS-H sec 2.6). Any map, tariff summary or note still showing Milk River as a Plains system is pre-2021. Inter Pipeline is itself Brookfield-controlled since November 2021 (WS-H Part 1 sec 2 headnote).

Three uses of one name: Milk River is (1) this pipeline, (2) the town and station in southern Alberta where the Bow River interconnect sits, and (3) the delivery name for the south end of the Bow River system. The nodes on this map keep them apart: Milk River Station is the place and the interconnect; the pipeline is the single edge south of it to the border.

History: the CER profile records commenced operations in 1970. The 2021 transaction was an asset swap, not a cash purchase: Plains received Inter Pipeline's Empress II (100 percent) and Empress V (50 percent) straddle-plant interests, and Inter Pipeline received approx C$35 million cash in the same transaction (WS-H sec 2.6, Grade C).

WITHHELD

Battery-level membership is not drawn and is not publicly derivable: in the held Petrinex test (June 2026), the battery-to-pipeline counterparty is confidentiality-suppressed on 78.9% of Alberta battery oil-disposition volume, and no Alberta battery row names a pipeline counterparty (WS-H Part 3). This map is drawn at system and interconnect grain; the SE Alberta catchment and its two feeder edges are INFERRED, never battery membership.

No monthly flow series is carried: flows_kbd is absent on every edge. The house egress record holds no CER monthly throughput series for this line: hidden_files/datasets/egress/empirical_stack_history.json records milk_river as a NAMEPLATE FALLBACK with zero months of CER throughput history on file, so there is no held monthly series to key by YYYY-MM. The only flow carried is a dated point, last_flow_kbd 90 (approx 90,000 b/d throughput at acquisition, vintage 2021, Inter Pipeline release via WS-H sec 2.6), printed with its vintage; nothing is interpolated from it and no current throughput is asserted.

Committed and uncommitted capacity are absent on the trunkline edge: the CER No. 178 toll rows in tolls.db (ids 242, 243) are both uncommitted service and no committed split or committed total is published for the Milk River Pipeline in the sources checked, so committed_kbd and uncommitted_kbd stay absent. The 32,500 b/d take-or-pay figure on the Bow River leg is a Bow River contract for that leg only; it is not a Milk River Pipeline commitment and is never carried across the interconnect.

Capacity is two published figures, not one averaged number: approx 97.9 thousand b/d (CER profile, October 2024, Grade A) is carried as nameplate_kbd, and the operator figure of approx 15,565 m3/d (approx 100,000 b/d, Grade B) is printed in the print-sheet rows. The balance model rounds the same line to 98 kb/d. No third figure is derived from them.

Length is two published figures, not one reconciled number: the CER profile shows CER-regulated length as 70 km (system including associated segments), while WS-H describes two approx 16 km pipelines and tolls.db path 49 is 16.5 km. Both are printed with their sources; the map draws the station-to-border segment only.

Bow River tolls are not cited: tolls.db (queried 2026-10-19) holds no Bow River pipeline, path or toll rows, so the Bow River edges carry a toll_note and no toll_refs. Bow River is AER-regulated and has no CER tariff; absence from tolls.db is a coverage and ingest item, never a market gap or an unpublished toll, and no toll number is invented.

Front Range geometry beyond the border is not drawn: delivery on this map ends at the Front Range interconnect at the border, the delivery point the CER profile names. The Front Range Pipeline in the United States and its Montana and Rockies refiner deliveries are a separate U.S. system, stated in text on the border node and the print sheet, not compiled as geometry here.

No separate Milk River gathering toll, receipt list or stream name is printed, because none exists in the record: the Milk River Pipeline has one receipt point (the Bow River interconnect) and carries a variety of crude oil streams (CER). Any receipt list, gathering toll or named Milk River stream would be invention.
