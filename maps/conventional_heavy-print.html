THE DURABLE NUMBERS

Anchor system
Husky Midstream General Partnership: Saskatchewan Gathering System (Lloydminster), approx 2,300 km in the Lloydminster region; operator Cenovus (35% owner, with Power Assets 48.75% and CK Infrastructure 16.25%)
WS-H Part 1 sec 3.1 (HMGP FY2023 report; Cenovus website)

WCS blending
Husky Midstream is responsible for blending Western Canadian Select (WCS) and describes itself as the exclusive WCS blender, at the Lloydminster and Hardisty end of its system
WS-H Part 1 sec 3.1 (HMGP FY2023 report; Cenovus website)

Husky system scale
Throughput stated as 250,000+ b/d; storage 5.9 MMbbl at Lloydminster and Hardisty combined (both figures system-wide, all HMGP assets)
Cenovus website, crawled 2026, via WS-H sec 3.1 and open item 11

Lloydminster Upgrader
Nameplate 81.5 kb/d (operable 78.5 kb/d); the adjacent Lloydminster Refinery (asphalt refinery) is a separate 29.0 kb/d nameplate
Cenovus downstream capacity disclosure update, 2024

Cold Lake system
Inter Pipeline Cold Lake Pipeline System: 1,200 kb/d current bitumen-blend capacity (1,900 kb/d ultimate), approx 604 kb/d committed, 621.3 kb/d throughput in 2020; deliveries to Edmonton and Hardisty, plus the Bruderheim rail lateral (up to 100 kb/d delivered, approx 320 kb/d ultimate)
Inter Pipeline AIF YE2020, via WS-H sec 2.4

Bow River system
Inter Pipeline: ultimate approx 180 kb/d, 2020 throughput approx 85.3 kb/d; segregated Hardisty to Milk River leg under take-or-pay for approx 32.5 kb/d to end-2026
Inter Pipeline AIF YE2020, via WS-H sec 2.1

Mid-Saskatchewan system
Inter Pipeline: ultimate approx 150.1 kb/d (Heavy Blend plus Light Sweet), 2020 throughput approx 47.1 kb/d, delivery to the Enbridge Mainline at Kerrobert
Inter Pipeline AIF YE2020, via WS-H sec 2.3

Manito System
Plains: heavy blend from the Manito, Bodo and Cactus Lake area to the PMC Kerrobert Terminal, with parallel diluent return lines; capacity not disclosed
WS-H Part 1 sec 1.2 and open item 2

Kerrobert diluent supply
Empress/Kerrobert Pipeline (Plains) carries natural gasoline diluent from Empress to Kerrobert for blending into crude at Kerrobert
Plains, 2020, via WS-H sec 1.5

Chauvin system (Gibson)
Chauvin, SK area to Hardisty: acquired from Teine Energy for C$400M, closed 2026-05-01, long-term take-or-pay and area-of-dedication; current effective capacity approx 30 kb/d, expansion to approx 45 kb/d expected to be sanctioned by end-2026; Hardisty Connection project sanctioned
Gibson release via GlobeNewswire, 2026-05-01, via WS-H sec 9.5

Gibson Hardisty network
Canadian crude pipeline network around Hardisty: combined throughput approx 90 kb/d across more than 500 km of pipelines; Hardisty Terminal tankage 13.5 MMbbl in recent investor material
Gibson operations pages and fact sheet, 2024-26, via WS-H sec 9.5

Nipisi and Mitsue (Pembina)
Nipisi: 190 km heavy-oil line, design 100 kb/d (expandable to approx 200 kb/d), Pelican Lake and Nipisi area to Edmonton via the Swan Hills / Judy Creek junction; Mitsue: diluent from Whitecourt, design 22 kb/d (expandable to 45 kb/d); both in service August 2011 on 10-year agreements
Pembina releases, 2010-11, via WS-H sec 9.1

Tolls
No toll is printed for any system on this map: no rows for these systems exist in tolls.db (queried 2026-10-19). Downstream Mainline paths from Hardisty and Kerrobert are in tolls.db (paths 2 and 3) and belong to the Mainline dataset
tolls.db query, 2026-10-19; WS-H Part 1

NOTES

OPEN ITEM (WS-H open item 11): how Peace River and Wabasca heavy oil historically associated with Rainbow is tendered at Edmonton today is not established in public sources. Enbridge's commodity map shows Wabasca Heavy (WH) into the Conventional Heavy pool and Seal Heavy (SH) as a segregated commodity, but neither is attributed to a feeder in any document consulted. No Peace River heavy route is drawn on this map; the supply area is shaded and the question is printed.

Name warning, printed on the map itself: there are two Saskatchewan Gathering Systems. Kingston's (SE Saskatchewan, light and medium, to Cromer) is on the Kingston dataset. Husky Midstream's (Lloydminster, heavy, Cenovus-operated, the WCS blender) is this map, and its system tag carries the (Lloydminster) qualifier on every edge.

Every Inter Pipeline capacity and throughput on this sheet is AIF YE2020 vintage (filed February 2021), the last public AIF before the Brookfield acquisition closed in November 2021. Brookfield-era Inter Pipeline publishes no equivalent AIF (WS-H open item 10); the figures are printed with their vintage, never as current.

This is the conventional heavy map: mined oil sands trunk systems (Corridor, Access, Waupisoo, Grand Rapids) are not drawn. Cold Lake is in-situ bitumen blend gathered by a conventional-style gathering and trunk system, and is included because the Tier 1 spec names Cold Lake explicitly; its oil-sands character is stated on its node and edges, not hidden.

WCS is blended here, not just priced here: Husky Midstream (Cenovus-operated) is the exclusive blender of Western Canadian Select, blending Lloydminster-area heavy feed at the Lloydminster and Hardisty end of its system (WS-H sec 3.1). Cold Lake Blend-family barrels entering Hardisty on the Cold Lake south leg enter that same WCS blending pool (WS-H sec 2.4).

Kerrobert is the west Saskatchewan Mainline door: two different owners (Inter Pipeline Mid-Saskatchewan and Plains Manito) deliver heavy blend at the same hub, on different systems, and the diluent that blends those barrels arrives at Kerrobert on the Empress/Kerrobert line and returns to the fields on the Manito system's parallel diluent lines. The map draws all three legs so the blending loop reads on the page.

Hardisty receives from four directions on this map: Husky Midstream from Lloydminster (north), the Cold Lake south leg (north), Bow River gathering from SE Alberta (south), and Gibson's Chauvin system from Saskatchewan (east). That convergence, not any single system, is why Hardisty is the heavy hub.

Labelled inference (WS-H): the Bow River equalization stream name at Hardisty is an open item (WS-H open item 8). Bow River gathered oil is delivered into Hardisty common streams; no separate Bow River Blend stream is named on this map, in contrast to the filing-established SES to LSB and MSM to Midale mappings on the Kingston map.

WITHHELD

Battery-level stream membership is not drawn and is not publicly derivable: in the held Petrinex test (June 2026), the battery-to-pipeline counterparty is confidentiality-suppressed on 87.9% of Saskatchewan and 78.9% of Alberta battery oil-disposition volume, and no Alberta battery row in June 2026 names a pipeline counterparty at all (WS-H Part 3). Receipt points are shown at system and named-area grain only; catchment shading and area feeder edges are INFERRED, never battery membership.

Husky Midstream receipt points are not named: WS-H sec 3.1 describes thermal and heavy batteries across the Lloydminster area, including Cenovus and Husky thermal projects, but names no individual receipt terminal or thermal project for the Saskatchewan Gathering System (Lloydminster). No project is plotted or assigned to the system here. A Saskatchewan Gathering System-only capacity is likewise not disclosed (WS-H open item 11): the 250,000+ b/d throughput and 5.9 MMbbl storage figures are system-wide across all Husky Midstream Lloydminster and Hardisty assets, and the throughput figure is carried on the Lloydminster Terminal node labelled as exactly that, never as a leg or terminal flow.

Peace River heavy routing is withheld as an open item, not drawn: heavy oil from the Peace River deposit (Seal and Cadotte country) and the Wabasca area was transported on Rainbow in the 2008 description, and Enbridge's commodity map shows Wabasca Heavy (WH) as a receipt commodity into the Conventional Heavy pool and Seal Heavy (SH) as a segregated commodity, but neither is attributed to Rainbow or to any other feeder in any document consulted (WS-H dossier 2C sec B3 and open item 11). Whether that heavy oil is blended into a south leg, tendered as WH at Edmonton, or now moves on Pembina's Nipisi and Mitsue systems instead is unresolved. The supply area is shaded and the open item is printed; no route is approximated.

Fosterton and the SW Saskatchewan heavy gathering country are not in this dataset: the WS-H dossier held for this build contains no Fosterton system entry, so no Fosterton collector can be mapped honestly from WS-H. Fosterton is Tier 1 item 5 in the maps inventory, a separate dataset; nothing for it is approximated here.

Cold Lake leg splits are withheld: Inter Pipeline's AIF YE2020 publishes system totals for the Cold Lake Pipeline System (1,200 kb/d current capacity, 1,900 kb/d ultimate, approx 604 kb/d committed, 621.3 kb/d throughput in 2020) but no Edmonton-leg versus Hardisty-leg split. The totals are carried once, on the Cold Lake node, and no leg edge carries a capacity. The superseded 2013-14 leg figures (650 kb/d to Edmonton, approx 220 kb/d to Hardisty) are not used and are not averaged with the AIF totals.

Manito System and South Saskatchewan System capacities are not disclosed in the sources checked (WS-H open item 2), so no capacity field appears on any Manito edge. The South Saskatchewan System itself (Plains heavy gathering to the Regina Terminal, WS-H sec 1.3) is not drawn: its confirmed delivery is Regina and the Mainline there, outside this map's Lloydminster, Hardisty, Cold Lake and northern-feeder geography, and its sourcing in WS-H is Grade C with old-vintage wording. It is named here so the omission is printed, not silent.

Committed and uncommitted splits are absent except where a source publishes them: Cold Lake system commitments (approx 604 kb/d, AIF YE2020, carried on the node with no published uncommitted split), the Bow River segregated take-or-pay leg (approx 32.5 kb/d to end-2026, carried on that edge), and Chauvin's take-or-pay and area-of-dedication form (carried as commitment_term with no committed kb/d, because Gibson publishes no committed volume). No other system here publishes a committed split, so committed_kbd and uncommitted_kbd stay absent on every other edge and node.

Tolls are not printed for any system on this map: tolls.db (queried 2026-10-19) holds no pipeline, path or toll rows for Husky Midstream, the Cold Lake, Bow River, Mid-Saskatchewan, Manito, Empress/Kerrobert, Chauvin or Nipisi systems, so no toll_refs or toll_note is cited. These are provincially regulated or commercial gathering systems whose tolls are not in the house tolls database; where a filed tariff exists for any of them, absence from tolls.db is an ingest backlog item, never a market gap, and no toll number is invented to fill it. The downstream Mainline paths from Hardisty (tolls.db path 2) and Kerrobert (path 3) exist in tolls.db but belong to the Mainline dataset, not to these gathering edges.

No monthly flow series (flows_kbd) is carried anywhere: no held monthly series exists for any system on this map. The only flow figures carried are dated published points, each with its vintage: Husky Midstream system-wide throughput stated as 250,000+ b/d (Cenovus website, crawled 2026, carried as 250 with its system-wide caveat), Cold Lake 2020 throughput 621.3 kb/d, Mid-Saskatchewan 2020 throughput 47.1 kb/d, and Bow River 2020 throughput 85.3 kb/d (all Inter Pipeline AIF YE2020). Nothing is interpolated between points, and post-2020 Inter Pipeline throughputs are not published in an equivalent AIF (WS-H open item 10).

Gibson Hardisty network detail is not itemized: Gibson publishes a combined figure for its Canadian crude pipeline network around Hardisty (combined throughput approx 90 kb/d across more than 500 km of 100%-owned pipelines, including the Viking Pipeline in service 2019) with no per-line split in the sources checked (WS-H sec 9.5 and open item 9). Only the Chauvin system, whose current effective capacity (approx 30 kb/d) and sanction-expected expansion (to approx 45 kb/d) Gibson does publish, is drawn as a Gibson edge; the rest of the Hardisty network is represented by the Hardisty hub node and the sheet rows, not by invented lines.

The Marten Hills / Clearwater heavy feed is not repeated here: Kingston's Marten Hills Pipeline System (60 kb/d) interconnects with the Plains Rainbow system at Mitsue for Edmonton access and is drawn on the Rainbow dataset (WS-H secs 9.3 and 2C). It is a Clearwater heavy feeder to Edmonton, not a Lloydminster, Hardisty or Peace River feeder, and drawing it twice would double-count the geography.

Two name collisions are printed so they cannot be misread: (1) Husky Midstream's Saskatchewan Gathering System (Lloydminster, heavy, Cenovus-operated) is a different system from Kingston Midstream's Saskatchewan Gathering System (SE Saskatchewan, light and medium, feeding Westspur to Cromer, on the Kingston dataset), and the system value on every Husky edge here carries the (Lloydminster) qualifier verbatim from WS-H for that reason; (2) Pembina's Nipisi Pipeline and Mitsue Pipeline on this map are not Plains' Nipisi Terminal and Mitsue Terminal on the Rainbow system, and this map's Nipisi Terminal namesake (the Nipisi / Pelican Lake area node) is Pembina's receipt country, not the Rainbow facility.
