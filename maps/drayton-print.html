NOTES

Edmonton only. There is no Drayton Valley to Hardisty leg, stream or delivery point - the map's job is partly to kill that misread.

North 40 takes sweet crude and condensate only - no low-sulphur crude (June 2026 delivery matrix).

Capacity is three filed numbers (145,000 / about 170,000 / 190,000-195,000 b/d) with no published reconciliation; all three are printed rather than one picked silently.

No Hardisty route: nothing in the June 2026 Drayton Valley toll schedule, the Pembina AIF description or the Enbridge Crude Oil Commodity Map (September 1, 2026) routes Drayton barrels to Hardisty. Drayton is an Edmonton light and condensate feeder; Hardisty is the heavy (Western Canadian Select) pricing point. Any note routing Drayton to Hardisty directly is wrong on the sources found (WS-H dossier 2D).

Edmonton only, with one exception inside Edmonton: Enbridge Edmonton Terminal and Trans Mountain each take sweet crude, low-sulphur crude and condensate; PKM North 40 takes sweet crude and condensate only - no low-sulphur crude (June 2026 delivery matrix).

Three names, one word: Pembina is (1) Pembina Pipeline Corporation, the midstream company, (2) the Pembina (Cardium) field at Drayton Valley that this system serves, and (3) Pembina's separate Peace Pipeline system. The Enbridge receipt list carries Pembina (P) for this Drayton Valley system; do not conflate it with Peace (WS-H).

The Brazeau NGL system is not this map: ethane-plus from the same gas plants moves on Pembina's Brazeau Pipeline to Fort Saskatchewan, segregated from Drayton crude and condensate tenders. Plant-recovered condensate (the tariff's White Products receipts, and Lateral 9 receipts coded to Keyera gas plants) does tender into Drayton as condensate (WS-H dossier 2D Part B.4).

Labelled inference (WS-H): Drayton sweet receipts delivering into Enbridge Edmonton enter the Edmonton sweet pool (the Mixed Sweet Blend (MSW) family, priced at Edmonton). The tariff does not name a downstream pool for low-sulphur receipts, and none is assigned on this map.

Founding asset: Pembina Pipe Line Ltd. was incorporated September 24, 1954 as the operator of a pipeline system within the Pembina field, built to move Drayton Valley crude to Edmonton. Drayton Valley is Pembina's original system, not a later acquisition (WS-H dossier 2D Part B.5).

WITHHELD

Battery-level membership is not drawn and no battery is assigned to this system: WS-H Part 3 tested the held Petrinex data (June 2026) and found the battery-to-pipeline counterparty suppressed on 78.9% of Alberta battery oil-disposition volume, with no Alberta battery row naming a pipeline counterparty. Receipt points are shown at tariff-grouping grain only (laterals and trunk groupings in the June 2026 toll schedule).

Capacity reconciliation is withheld, not resolved: the system carries three filed figures with no published reconciliation - 145,000 b/d (Pembina AIF YE2023), about 170,000 b/d (AIF YE2024) and 190,000-195,000 b/d (completed 2012 Calmar expansion). The nameplate carried on the hub node and the trunk edge is the AIF YE2023 figure, the number WS-H dossier 2D uses as current; the other two are printed in the print-sheet rows, not silently dropped.

Committed and uncommitted capacity are absent: Pembina discloses Drayton Valley service as generally interruptible (AIF YE2023) and publishes no committed split, so committed_kbd and uncommitted_kbd stay absent on every edge. commitment_term on the trunk edge carries the interruptible disclosure itself.

Current throughput and any monthly flow series are absent: no Drayton Valley flow series is published in the sources consulted, so flows_kbd is absent on every edge and no last_flow_kbd is carried. The last flow figure in WS-H is Q1 2012 (transporting 130,000 b/d against 145,000 b/d capacity, Pembina Q1 2012 reporting) - history, not a current flow.

toll_refs are absent on every edge: tolls.db (queried 2026-10-19) carries no Drayton Valley pipeline or path, so there is no tolls.id to cite. That is an ingest backlog item, never a market gap: the tolls are published in Pembina's own posted Drayton Valley Petroleum Toll Schedule (effective June 1, 2026), cited in the trunk edge toll_note with its receipt-point ranges.

Cardium-specific quality is not printed: no primary or public API gravity or sulphur assay for Pembina Cardium oil was found in the WS-H pass. The Edmonton Mixed Sweet Blend (MSW) pool average (42.0 API, 0.42 wt% sulphur, CrudeMonitor five-year average to May 8, 2026) is the pool the sweet barrels enter, not the field's assay, and is not carried as a field number.

The downstream pool for Drayton low-sulphur receipts is not assigned: the June 2026 tariff creates the low-sulphur category but does not name its Edmonton downstream pool, and WS-H leaves the confirmation against the Enbridge commodity map as an open item.

Condensate routing beyond the tariff is not drawn: the tariff confirms condensate is a Drayton product with Edmonton delivery, and no separate condensate geometry is shown. The RBN diluent-trail account of Drayton lease condensate reaching Pembina's Edmonton terminal and the Enbridge condensate (CRW) network was not verified in the WS-H pass (flagged there as an open item) and is not relied on here.
