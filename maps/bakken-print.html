NOTES

The two doors print on the map face: Door 1 (Enbridge ND) delivers at Clearbrook, the pricing point where the Bakken (UHC) differential is made. Door 2 (Bakken North, plus Saskatchewan Westspur gathering) injects at Cromer, the injection point and apportionment gauge, and rides Line 65 south through Gretna to Clearbrook.

Line width ranks the exits: width follows the published flow where one exists and capacity where only capacity is published. Every label states which it is.

Proposed and converted lines are drawn as what they are: Bridger Expansion dashed amber (in permitting, no FID), Double H greyed (out of crude service 2025, NGL from April 2026). Neither is in the egress stack.

Montana refining (about 210 kb/d) is contested memo demand and stays out of the panel: whether the Billings, Laurel, Great Falls and Cut Bank refineries run Bakken barrels at the margin is not established.

Kerrobert, at the map's northwest corner, is upstream Mainline context (a Saskatchewan injection and apportionment point on Lines 2 and 93), not Bakken supply; it carries no row in this panel.

The two Enbridge doors, because prose always muddles them. Door 1: the Enbridge North Dakota line runs straight into Clearbrook, and Clearbrook is the pricing point: the Bakken differential (the UHC differential) is made there. Door 2: Bakken North runs north into Cromer, and Cromer is an injection point and the Mainline apportionment gauge, not a pricing point. What enters at Cromer, Bakken North barrels plus the larger Saskatchewan Westspur gathering flow, rides Mainline Line 65 (186 kb/d) south through Gretna and down through Clearbrook, so Cromer barrels price off the same Clearbrook number. Getting on at Cromer and pricing at Clearbrook are different events, and the map keeps them separate.

County shading is production share, not a boundary. The four core counties print 92% of North Dakota's August 2026 well-file runs: McKenzie 385.5 kb/d, Williams 289.6, Mountrail 201.7, Dunn 195.5. County lines are not drawn; the shaded ellipses mark where the barrels are, and the panel carries the state total.

Field-pools: Hofflund (47.2 kb/d, August 2026) is labelled. The Elidah (42.6 kb/d) and Little Knife (28.2 kb/d) dots sit in western McKenzie County and in Dunn County; their labels are omitted at this frame because the basin patch is too dense to carry them without covering the hub labels, and the figures print here and in the book's Production table instead. All three are Bakken pool, NDIC well file, Runs basis.

Flows print only where a source publishes them. Dakota Access carries the basin's one measured trunk flow (611 kb/d, FERC Form 6, calendar 2024). Every other pipe prints capacity, and the label says capacity.

Butte prints both of its figures because they disagree: the filed nameplate is 150 kb/d, and 2025 actuals ran about 317 to 340 kb/d after Double H barrels moved onto the line (East Daley's read of the FERC filings; the drag-reducing-agent mechanism is their inference, printed as such in the book).

Double H left crude service in 2025 and entered NGL service on April 29, 2026 (Hiland Express). It is drawn as history, not as a live crude route, and it is out of the book's 1,259 kb/d egress stack.

Bridger Pipeline Expansion (about 550 kb/d, 36-inch, border to Guernsey) is in permitting with no final investment decision on file in the book's company sweep. It is drawn dashed and is not in the egress stack.

The basin takes in no crude: the book's imports line is 0.0 kb/d, and the map prints that as a fact rather than implying a two-way system. Guernsey also receives Canadian barrels on the Platte line from Casper; that is an inflow to the junction, not to the basin, and it is named in the Guernsey node note rather than drawn as a basin inflow.

Manitoba memo: the conventional pools of southwestern Manitoba, on the Williston basin's northeast flank around Virden, produce about 41 kb/d (Manitoba Petroleum Branch Oil and Gas Statistics, calendar 2025 average of 6,508 cubic metres per day). They are conventional Williston pools, not Bakken play; they clear east from the Cromer area on the Mainline; and they are outside the basin supply frame and the panel total, carried as a memo line only.

Storage: no public Bakken or Clearbrook stock series exists, so storage change is the book's residual. It is bounded by about 2.0 million barrels of northern-hub tank shell (Clearbrook about 1.3 million barrels, Berthold about 0.7).

Rail loadouts drawn are the principal terminals (Epping COLT, Trenton, New Town, Stanley, Dickinson). Legacy loadout nameplate exceeded 1 million barrels per day and most of it is idle or in preservation; current loadings are the EIA July 2026 print of about 170 kb/d, which is why rail is the swing outlet: it is the exit that opens and closes with the differential.

Montana refining (Billings, Laurel, Great Falls, Cut Bank; about 210 kb/d on the EIA January 2026 survey) is contested memo demand. The cluster is drawn at Billings and marked memo; it is not in the balance.

Upstream context, west of Cromer: the Mainline corridor continues west through Regina to Kerrobert, Saskatchewan, a Mainline light injection point in its own right (Lines 2 and 93). The October 2026 apportionment notice printed Light 19 percent at Kerrobert, a separate gauge from Cromer's 13 percent. Saskatchewan sweets (MSY from the Smiley and Kindersley area, and MSE, mid-Saskatchewan) inject at Kerrobert and pool into the MSW stream on Enbridge. Kerrobert is context for the river that runs Cromer to Clearbrook; it is not Bakken supply and no Kerrobert volume is carried in the panel.

WITHHELD

Monthly flows by pipeline: no public source publishes them. Form 6 is annual, and the monthly split in every public treatment is modelled, so this map prints flows only at Form 6 and EIA grain.

A Bakken crude stock series: none is published (EIA carries no Bakken or Clearbrook series). Storage prints as the residual, never as a measured line.

Confidential-well pool detail: NDIC masks the pool on tight-hole wells for about six months, so field-pool labels print printed pools only; about 134.6 kb/d of August 2026 runs sit in masked wells and are allocated in the book, not mapped.

Method: Facts compiled per node and per line from the sources named on each element: NDIC well-file rollups (county and field-pool, August 2026, Runs basis), MBOGC county well files (Montana Bakken, July 2026), Saskatchewan Ministry pool reports (Viewfield, July 2026), FERC Form 6 annuals (Dakota Access calendar 2024 flow), EIA crude-by-rail (July 2026) and EIA-820 refinery capacity (January 1, 2026), CER pipeline profiles and Enbridge system factsheets (capacities), and the Bakken book's carried figures. Nodes sit at gazetteer coordinates of the named places. Routes are schematic corridors between true endpoints through waypoints on or near the published route; the map face carries the standing label that routes are approximate and nodes are at true locations. Line width ranks the exits: width follows the published flow where one exists (Dakota Access 611 kb/d is the anchor) and capacity where only capacity is published. Flows and nameplate are always distinguished in the label text, never by implication. Geometry is Heavy Reading's own drawing from open sources (state and provincial open data, EIA public domain, CER open data); no third-party map was copied or traced.
