NOTES

Everything inland crude wants is on this coast: the refineries take the first cut at about 6.9 million barrels a day of capacity, the pricing points (East Houston for WTI Houston, ECHO for ICE WTI Midland) are set where the pipes land, and the export docks take whatever the refineries do not. When the docks are the marginal bid, inland prices are set by the water.

Corpus Christi is the sharp end: the shortest run to deep water, the Enbridge Ingleside terminal loading over 1.1 million barrels a day through 2025, and a port complex that loaded at a 2.4 to 2.5 million barrel a day pace in March 2026, roughly half of all United States crude exports.

Line width ranks the doors: width follows the published flow where one exists and capacity where only capacity is published. Every label states which it is.

Two pricing points sit inside this one refining centre, and they price different things. East Houston (the ONEOK terminal) is where WTI Houston is priced, the waterborne-adjacent grade; ECHO (Enterprise's terminal) is the ICE WTI Midland delivery point, where Permian barrels delivered by pipe settle. The spread between them is the market's running read on how badly the coast wants inland barrels this week.

The coast's arithmetic is the lesson. About 6,900 kb/d of refining is drawn on this map, and about 7,800 kb/d of inbound pipeline capacity lands here from the Permian and Cushing alone, before Eagle Ford barrels and waterborne imports. Supply structurally exceeds the refineries' appetite, so the excess has exactly one place to go: the export docks. That is why Corpus Christi, with the shortest deep water run, became the top crude export port in the country.

LOOP is drawn at the frame's edge on purpose. It is the only berth in the United States that fully loads a VLCC, it stores crude in salt caverns at Clovelly, and its LOCap line feeds St. James and the Mississippi River refineries. It belongs to this coast's story even though its crude mostly arrives by water.

Waterborne crude imports by terminal: EIA publishes imports by customs district and port at monthly grain (carried in the site's Census district file), not by individual terminal, so no import figure is printed per dock.

Ship Channel draft: Houston loads to Suezmax class on a 45 foot channel, and only LOOP fully loads a VLCC. The constraint is printed on the LOOP node rather than drawn as a separate layer.
