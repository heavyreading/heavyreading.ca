NOTES

Basis statement, printed in full: quantities on this sheet match the Now page levels strip basis. The strip's supply figure and egress capacity are the same quantity type (marketable supply: Alberta removals from ST3 including blending diluent, plus 600 kb/d of Saskatchewan and other supply, against crude-only egress capacity). Alberta gross field production (4,245 kb/d in the October 2026 balance month) is a different quantity and is never printed beside egress capacity on this map or sheet.

Per-system basis exceptions: (1) Mainline design nameplate (3,200 kb/d) is a gross figure including non-crude space; its marketable-basis stack figure is 3,050 kb/d and both are printed. (2) Keystone committed volume (585 kb/d) sits on the South Bow AIF 591 kb/d nominal basis while its stack capacity (643 kb/d) sits on the egress basis; the two are never added or netted against each other here. (3) Rangeland's 45 kb/d is the corridor capacity of the binding Aurora border segment and carries NGLs and light oil, so the pipe stack total includes one small mixed leg, as the house stack itself carries it. (4) The inbound condensate nameplates (180 and 110 kb/d) are import capacities on their own basis and are excluded from the egress stack total.

Intended placement: the Now page egress section (a proposal the coordinator carries; this dataset does not edit the Now page).

Spare reads on this map are the balance's modelled spare (stack top minus marketable supply), not a meter reading: October 2026 prints +66 kb/d, thin but positive, widening as the 2027 capacity steps land.

Stack view, not a route atlas: each trunk system is one edge family at corridor fidelity. The Trans Mountain leg deliberately carries no system detail beyond the stack quantities; the Trans Mountain dataset owns that geography. Line 9 is not drawn: it moves barrels already east of the basin (Sarnia to Montreal) and is not WCSB egress; its map is the Line 9 dataset.

The two inbound condensate legs (Southern Lights, Cochin) are drawn into the basin on purpose: diluent supply is part of the egress picture, because imported diluent leaves Alberta again inside blended exports and the balance's marketable supply series already captures it (ST3 removals identity; docs/EGRESS_METHODS.md). Their nameplates (180 and 110 kb/d) are import capacities and are never added to the crude egress stack total.

Mainline nameplate discipline: the design nameplate is 3,200 kb/d gross, of which 215 kb/d is space the stack reserves for non-crude products, leaving a 2,985 kb/d crude nameplate; the stack carries 3,050 kb/d because CER crude throughput, crude-adjusted, has demonstrated more than the crude nameplate. Both figures are printed in the sheet so the basis is never blended silently.

Trans Mountain flows are crude only: the CER delivery-point sum counts domestic heavy and light crude at Westridge, Sumas and Burnaby and excludes refined petroleum products moving on the same system (46 kb/d in June 2026), which is why the last flow here (819 kb/d, June 2026) sits below the all-products delivery total (866 kb/d) carried in the egress watch.

Capacity steps already in the stack schedule (builders/egress_data.py): Trans Mountain drag reducing agent +90 kb/d from January 2027 (stack 955.6 kb/d on the proven-floor basis; nameplate 980 kb/d), Enbridge Mainline Optimization Phase 1 +150 kb/d from September 2027 (3,350 kb/d gross nameplate), Express +30 kb/d from 2028 (340 kb/d, Southern Illinois Connector), Trans Mountain Mainline Optimization Project +210 kb/d from October 2028 (1,190 kb/d). The nameplate_kbd fields on this map are the October 2026 stack, before those steps.

System naming: edge system keys are the balance model's own egress-stack keys verbatim (mainline, trans_mountain, keystone, express, milk_river, rangeland, from builders/site/build_egress_outlook.py PIPE_STACK and builders/egress_data.py), plus southern_lights and cochin from the C5 dataset for the diluent import legs. Crude by rail has no stack key in the model (it is the stack's rail layer, not a pipe), so its edges carry system: rail, named in this note rather than borrowed from a pipe key.

Forward flows (October 2026 to December 2027) on the six pipe edges are the house balance's emitted per-system split (hidden_files/datasets/egress/egress_split_monthly.json, emitted by builders/egress_split.py; method note: references/egress_split_method_2026-10-19.md in the goal workspace): rail first at the economic 85 kb/d; Express / Milk River / Rangeland at their doctrine figures (Express 290 kb/d, a proven floor from sustained average shipments in the first half of 2026; Milk River 90 kb/d, a proven floor from operator-stated throughput in 2021; Rangeland 45 kb/d, the corridor capacity of the binding Aurora border segment - no observed shipment series exists for the line; directed 2026-10-19, basis in the split method note); and the remainder across Mainline / Trans Mountain / Keystone pro-rata to the same empirical effective capacities the balance caps against (equal utilization - in months below the swing lines' combined capacity no line is pinned at its cap; in the balance's capped months, November and December 2026, every pipe prints at effective capacity). The split is an allocation of the balance's egress total: the balance is the forecast; the split is arithmetic on it. Each month the six pipes plus rail sum to the balance's marketable total (exports plus 600 kb/d Saskatchewan and other supply) exactly. Basis step at the forecast boundary, stated plainly: observed Mainline flows are CER gross ex-Gretna throughput, which includes the 215 kb/d of non-crude space; forecast Mainline flows are the balance's crude-basis allocation, because the stack reserves that 215 kb/d off the Mainline, so the Mainline series steps down by roughly the non-crude space where observation ends and the model begins. Forecast months are model output; the balance vintage is carried in the split artifact.

WITHHELD

Per-system forecast flows are the house balance's emitted split (from October 2026; see Notes): model output on the balance basis, summing to the balance's marketable total each month. Two splits remain withheld: the crude-by-rail total is a single basin leg with no sourced split across the three drawn rail corridors, so no rail edge carries flows_kbd; and the balance does not divide any pipe's flow between Alberta and Saskatchewan/other barrels, so no per-pipe origin split is drawn.

Express, Milk River and Rangeland flow HISTORY is withheld: the house CER throughput file carries no throughput history for these three lines (the empirical stack carries them at the labelled figures in hidden_files/datasets/egress/empirical_stack_history.json (proven floors for Express and Milk River; corridor capacity for Rangeland)), so no observed series is drawn rather than a borrowed or estimated one. Their flows_kbd from October 2026 is the balance split's baseload leg, carried at the doctrine figures (Express 290 kb/d, proven floor from first-half 2026 sustained average shipments; Milk River 90 kb/d, proven floor from operator-stated 2021 throughput; Rangeland 45 kb/d corridor capacity of the binding Aurora segment - no observed series exists for the line; basis in the split method note), and is model output, not observation. In the balance's capped months (November and December 2026) the baseload legs print at effective capacity with the rest of the stack.

Southern Lights flows are withheld as a monthly series: the house CER Southern Lights file carries quarterly snapshots only (March, June, September and December points, 2011 to 2025), which is not a monthly-complete series, and quarterly points are not relabelled as monthly flows. The C5 dataset carries the system's capacity and commitment record.

Crude by rail is drawn without per-destination capacity or flow: the house record supports a system figure only (an 85 kb/d economic rail layer in the egress stack; CER crude by rail ran 88 kb/d in May 2026 and peaked at 412 kb/d in February 2020), and CER reporting carries no destination split the stack can cite, so the three schematic arrows carry no kb/d figures.

Keystone committed volume (585 kb/d) is carried on the South Bow AIF nominal basis (591 kb/d), not on the 640 kb/d egress stack nameplate basis, and no uncommitted_kbd is printed for Keystone: the AIF's 35 kb/d spot reservation and the 585 kb/d commitment are both AIF-basis figures and are not blended with stack-basis capacity. Express, Milk River and Rangeland committed splits are absent: no committed versus uncommitted split for these systems is published in the house record, so none is printed.

Trans Mountain committed tolls are absent from the edge toll fields: tolls.db carries only the superseded November 2023 preliminary interim benchmark for committed service (tolls.id 197, superseded), so no committed toll is printed as effective. The edge cites the effective interim uncommitted tolls (Interim Tariff No. 123).

Enbridge Mainline heavy uncommitted tolls on the Canadian segment are cited through the filed International Joint Tariff instead of a Canadian local row: tolls.db carries no effective heavy row for paths 1 and 2 under CER No. 600 (the heavy rows on file are superseded). Per the standing tolls rule, every toll is published in a filed tariff; a missing row is an ingest backlog item, never a market gap.

Rangeland tolls are absent: tolls.db carries no Rangeland pipeline path or toll rows (queried 2026-10-19 via python sqlite3), so no toll_refs are cited and no toll value is invented. Filed tariffs are published by the CER and FERC; absence from tolls.db is an ingest backlog item, never a market gap.

Milk River and Rangeland stack figures rest on their own bases, stated per line: Milk River carries its proven floor of 90 kb/d (operator-stated throughput at acquisition, 2021) against a 97.9 kb/d CER nameplate; the residual is unproven headroom; Rangeland carries 45 kb/d, the corridor capacity of the CER Aurora border segment (the corridor's binding segment), corroborated by CER Canada's Energy Future 2026 Appendix 4 (453 Mb/d combined for Express, Milk River and Aurora/Rangeland). Neither line has CER throughput history on file, so neither stack figure is an observed-maximum print.

Method: Facts compiled, expression ours (2026-10-17). Every node is a real place at gazetteer coordinates; a node's existence and role are sourced per node. Edges connect nodes through corridor waypoints on or near the published route (Canada Energy Regulator (CER) pipeline profiles and North American Cooperation on Energy Information (NACEI) geometry class); edge sources name the authority for the connection. This is the stack view: one edge family per egress system, not a route atlas, and the Trans Mountain leg carries stack quantities only (system detail lives in the Trans Mountain dataset). Capacities are the house egress stack on its marketable-supply basis (see sheet notes): per system, the higher of crude nameplate and the highest crude volume actually shipped in CER throughput data, with 215 kb/d of Enbridge Mainline space reserved for non-crude products taken off the Mainline leg. Flows (flows_kbd) are CER observed monthly throughput, January 2018 to June 2026, from the house CER throughput file (hidden_files/datasets/cer_throughput.csv, retrieved 2026-10-04 from the CER pull of 2026-09-22); June 2026 is the last actual month in that file. The house balance (builders/build_sd_balance.py; hidden_files/woodmack_storage/sd_balance.json and egress_monthly.json) forecasts TOTAL marketable supply and total exports forward (balance window October 2026 to December 2027; egress outlook to 2030) as model output, but it carries no per-system forecast split, so no per-system forecast flow is printed: any split would be an invented allocation, and the honesty rule forbids it. The forward totals are carried in the print sheet rows with their vintage instead, so every flow on this map is an observed actual carrying the CER vintage, and every forward number is labelled model output where it appears.
