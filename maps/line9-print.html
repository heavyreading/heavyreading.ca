NOTES

Two-shipper line: with no other shippers, the CER Line 9 system total is effectively Suncor plus Valero's combined inland draw, which is what makes the CER series the inland leg of the East Coast lights toggle.

The roughly 90 kb/d waterborne figure is an aggregate floor for the pair, not a per-plant requirement: either plant could in principle run a given month entirely off Line 9, and the split above the floor moves with price.

CER flow vintage warning: the Line 9 throughput file stops at Dec 2025, about ten months stale. The map prints history through Dec 2025 and no live flow; the live leg is AIS.

Ground truth overrides, printed deliberately: (1) commitments of about 130 kb/d each are firm rights, not caps, and the pair are the only two shippers (house ground truth, 2026-10-14); contemporary published accounts frame Suncor's 130 Mb/d as an expected maximum take, which the ground truth supersedes. (2) Suncor Montreal's run rate is printed as 150-155 kb/d (House ground truth) rather than the published 137 kb/d nameplate class figure carried by CER, the Canadian Fuels Association and the maps inventory, or the 157,000 b/d on Suncor's own refinery page. (3) Valero Levis nameplate is printed as 235 kb/d per the watch record and Valero's own description, not the 265 kb/d in RBN and Canadian Fuels Association tables. (4) The Portland Montreal Pipe Line is excluded as commercially dead since Dec 2015 (House ground truth), although Suncor's public refinery page still lists it as a supply channel.

No structural marine leg: Line 9 can move 300+ kb/d, more than either plant's full runs, so marine supply at both Montreal and Levis is an economic channel. The only structural bound is combined: about 390 kb/d of combined runs against about 300 kb/d of line leaves an aggregate waterborne floor of about 90 kb/d (House ground truth, 2026-10-14).

The river shuttle carries Line 9 crude, not marine supply: Stena Poseidon and Palva move Valero's inland barrels from Montreal East to Levis, the loaded downbound leg is the counted leg, and in the East Coast lights watch these barrels classify as inland pull (scoping memo Addendum 3, 2026-10-15).

Line 9's effective rate depends on the moving slate: heavier batches cost horsepower and pull the achievable rate below the 300 kb/d nameplate, and Suncor manages its heavy intake at Montreal by buffering with synthetic crude (House ground truth, 2026-10-14). The nameplate printed is the CER available capacity figure, not a slate adjusted rate.

System naming: the egress stack key list in SCHEMA.md (mainline, trans_mountain, keystone, express, milk_river, rangeland) does not include Line 9, so the pipeline edges carry the plain system name verbatim, system: Line 9, matching tolls.db pipeline 11 (Enbridge Line 9) and the CER file's pipeline name. The marine edges carry their own system labels (St. Lawrence river shuttle; St. Lawrence marine imports) and their own line keys.

Portland Montreal Pipe Line (PMPL) is deliberately absent from the drawing: commercially dead since Dec 2015 (House ground truth, 2026-10-14).

WITHHELD

Current Line 9 flows are withheld: the CER Line 9 throughput file held in the house stack stops at Dec 2025, about ten months stale at compilation, while sibling CER throughput files run to mid 2026. The flows_kbd series printed here ends at Dec 2025 (285 kb/d) and this map claims no live flow; the live inland leg of the East Coast lights watch is AIS observed, not CER.

The split of Line 9 deliveries between Suncor Montreal, Valero's Montreal East terminal and the North Westover delivery point is withheld: the CER file is system level only and publishes no delivery point split. The two committed volumes (about 130 kb/d each) are House ground truth, not a CER split.

Grade split is withheld: the CER Line 9 series is an aggregate of heavy, medium and light crude with no grade split, and no grade share is drawn. Suncor's heavy intake at Montreal is episodic (discrete WCS or Cold Lake batches buffered with SYN), not a fixed share (House ground truth, 2026-10-14).

Suncor Montreal's exact Line 9 versus marine split is withheld: it is a residual (CER total, less en route deliveries, less Valero terminal receipts inferred from shuttle loadings) and is publishable only as a labelled estimate, never as an observation (East Coast lights watch scoping memo, 2026-10-14). No split is drawn on this map.

Committed tolls are absent: tolls.db path 43 carries only the uncommitted tolls of Interim CER No. 587 (tolls.id 224, 225, 226). No committed toll row is carried in the database, so none is printed; per the standing tolls rule, that is an ingest state, not evidence about what is filed.

Montreal East terminal and St-Romuald berth throughput capacities are absent: no capacity for either marine terminal is carried in the watch record or the sources compiled, so none is printed.

The Portland Montreal Pipe Line (PMPL) is not drawn and no PMPL capacity is carried: it has been commercially dead since Dec 2015 (House ground truth, 2026-10-14). Suncor's public refinery page still lists PMPL among the refinery's supply channels; the ground truth governs and the line stays off the map.
