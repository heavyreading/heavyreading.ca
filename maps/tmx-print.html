THE DURABLE NUMBERS

System
Trans Mountain Pipeline System, Trans Mountain Pipeline ULC (Trans Mountain Corporation, federal Crown corporation)
CER Pipeline Profile; house pipe profile

Route
Edmonton, Alberta to Burnaby, British Columbia via Hinton, the Jasper corridor, Kamloops, Merritt and Hope; Sumas spur at Port Kells (Surrey) to Abbotsford; Puget Sound leg Sumas to Laurel, Washington and on to Ferndale, Cherry Point and Anacortes
CER Pipeline Profile (geometry backbone); Trans Mountain Puget Sound Pipeline page

System capacity
890 kb/d nominal (expanded system, commercial service May 1, 2024); pre-expansion 300 kb/d; expansion added 590 kb/d
CER Pipeline Profile; CER settlement release, September 28, 2026

Line split (operating)
Line 1 about 350 kb/d (light crude and refined products, land destinations); Line 2 about 540 kb/d (primarily heavy crude, Westridge dock)
Trans Mountain Q1 2025 presentation, via house pipe profile

Committed split
707.5 kb/d committed (79.5% of 890 kb/d) and 182.5 kb/d uncommitted; 15 and 20 year ship-or-pay, 93% at 20 years, 2.5% annual escalation
NEB RH-001-2012 firm total and Trans Mountain Q1 2025 presentation; CER, September 3, 2025 (via house pipe profile and commitments grid)

Contracting change
Firm cap rises from 80% to 90% of nominal capacity (about 801 kb/d) effective January 1, 2027 under the CER-approved tolls settlement
CER decision, September 28, 2026 (Filing C41001); house egress research notes (2026 open season oversubscribed)

System length
About 1,180 km, Edmonton to Burnaby (CER settlement release); CER profile regulated length 1,492 km as printed in the profile table
CER, September 28, 2026; CER Pipeline Profile

Westridge dock
3 berths (expanded from 1), Aframax class; dock maximum 630 kb/d, about 34 partially laden Aframax loadings a month
Trans Mountain project record, via house research brief; Trans Mountain via Reuters and World Pipelines, April 2024

Westridge loadings
CER Guide BB loadings averaged 529.4 kb/d in June 2026, the highest month in the held series (May 2024 to June 2026); about 75% of crude loaded is heavy
House Narrows record (narrows/data/westridge_monthly.json, CER Guide BB); heavy share per house research brief

Puget Sound Pipeline
111 km (69 miles), 16 to 20 inch, up to about 240 kb/d depending on product mix and delivery balance; in service since 1954; four Washington refineries (Cherry Point, Ferndale, and two at Anacortes)
Trans Mountain, Puget Sound Pipeline page

System throughput (latest held)
865.5 kb/d in June 2026 (CER system throughput, committed plus uncommitted); Q2 2026 averaged 840 kb/d, 94% utilization, with 510 kb/d to Westridge and 234 kb/d to Washington via Puget Sound
Held CER throughput file (conversion 6.28981 bbl per m3); Trans Mountain Q2 2026 results, via house deep dive

Tolls (interim, May 1, 2026)
Uncommitted, Edmonton origin, Interim Tariff No. 123: Westridge heavy C$14.06/bbl, Westridge light C$13.99/bbl, Burnaby light C$13.05/bbl, Sumas heavy C$12.53/bbl, Sumas light C$12.47/bbl, Kamloops light C$9.31/bbl (tank metered)
tolls.db ids 185 to 196 (pipeline id 7, paths 34 to 37), queried 2026-10-19

Products
Crude oil (heavy, light, synthetic) and refined petroleum products in batches; the only pipeline in North America batching refined products and crude in one system; heavy pools on Line 2 are Pacific Dilbit and Pacific Cold Lake
CER Pipeline Profile (transported commodity); house pipe profile grades section

NOTES

The 890 kb/d is a system figure for the twinned pair. It is carried on every trunk segment and is never a segment capacity.

CER is the Westridge volume record (monthly, in arrears); AIS is the live berth record (Narrows watch, from October 2026). The sheet prints CER volumes; vessel counts belong to the Narrows page.

All tolls on this sheet are interim. The settlement framework takes effect January 1, 2027; its tariff is a pending ingest in tolls.db and no successor toll is printed here.

TMX is a label, never a key: every system edge in this dataset carries system trans_mountain, the balance model egress-stack key for Trans Mountain. The expansion project (commercial service May 1, 2024, plus 590 kb/d) is the reason the nameplate reads 890 kb/d; the pre-expansion system was 300 kb/d.

Two lines, one system: Line 1 (1953) serves primarily land destinations in British Columbia and Washington state with light crude and refined products; Line 2 serves primarily the Westridge dock with heavier crude (Trans Mountain Q1 and Q2 2025 presentations, via house pipe profile). The map draws the twinned pair as one corridor, which is the atlas convention for this system.

The loadings geography on this page: CER reports Westridge volumes monthly in arrears (Guide BB filings; the house Narrows record holds May 2024 to June 2026), while the Narrows AIS watch counts berth visits live from October 2026. CER is the volume record; AIS is the timing, identity and destination record. No free historical AIS exists for Canadian waters, so history is CER and live is AIS, and the two are never spliced into one series.

Westridge draft constraint: Aframax tankers load partially laden at the Second Narrows draft. The house Narrows record carries the draft ladder (about 575,000 bbl average and about 620,000 bbl maximum per cargo at current draft, as captioned on the Westridge chart); the dock maximum of 630 kb/d and 34 partially laden Aframax a month is the operator figure (Trans Mountain, via Reuters, April 2024).

Sumas binds first: the Sumas delivery point has run at capacity since the expansion entered service (CER, September 3, 2025), and the CER Sumas key point sits at or near the Puget Sound 240 kb/d ceiling in most months of the held series. When the Puget leg apportions, Washington-bound barrels reroute to Westridge and move by tanker instead.

Growth options are not capacity: drag-reducing agent optimization (about plus 90 kb/d, targeted by early 2027) and the Mainline Optimization Project (about plus 210 kb/d by end of 2028, CER application filed August 27, 2026) would take the system toward about 1.19 million b/d, but neither is in the 890 kb/d nameplate carried here. Both are company and filing figures stated in the house pipe profile, not operating capacity.

Edmonton receipt point only: the Edmonton node is where this system ties to the Mainline and oil-sands supply side. No other system is drawn on this map, by design; the tie is at node level.

WITHHELD

Committed (firm) toll rows are absent from tolls.db: the database (queried 2026-10-19, python sqlite3) carries only the interim uncommitted rows of Interim Tariff No. 123 for Trans Mountain (toll ids 185 to 196), plus the superseded 2023 benchmark row (id 197). Firm tolls are published in the same tariff and are tabulated in the house pipe profile; their absence here is an ingest backlog item, never an unpublished toll, so no committed toll_refs are cited.

The successor settlement tariff effective January 1, 2027 is a known pending ingest: the CER approved the Trans Mountain tolls settlement on September 28, 2026 (Filing C41001, firm contracting 80% to 90%), but no successor-tariff rows exist in tolls.db yet. Every toll cited in this dataset is therefore the interim Tariff No. 123 value with its May 1, 2026 vintage stated in toll_note.

No toll is cited for the Puget Sound Pipeline segment south of Sumas: tolls.db carries no Puget Sound path. The Edmonton to Sumas filed toll (tolls.db path 36) is cited on the Sumas spur edge, and the U.S. leg is a FERC common-carrier segment whose tariff is outside the tolls.db scope as held.

Per-shipper committed volumes are not carried: individual Trans Mountain transportation service agreements are confidential. Only the aggregate is public (707,500 b/d firm from the 2012 open season rounds, NEB RH-001-2012; Trans Mountain Q1 2025 disclosures), and that aggregate is what the trunk edge carries. Company-disclosed single-shipper volumes held in the house commitments grid (CNRL, Cenovus, MEG) are shipper facts, not system splits, and are not drawn.

No per-segment nameplate is published for the twinned trunk: the 890 kb/d on each trunk segment is the combined Line 1 plus Line 2 system figure (about 350 plus about 540 kb/d operating split, Trans Mountain Q1 2025). Segment capacities are absent rather than divided.

No fixed branch split of the Puget Sound 240 kb/d is published: Trans Mountain states the capacity depends on petroleum types and the balance of deliveries between Anacortes and Ferndale, so the Laurel delivery branches carry no nameplate_kbd.

Puget Sound firm-service commitments are not published: no public firm-service evidence exists for the Puget Sound leg (house commitments grid), so committed_kbd is absent on every Puget edge rather than estimated from the Sumas flow series.

Historical Westridge loadings before May 2024 are not drawn as a series: the expanded-system loadings record held by the house starts with the expansion (CER Guide BB, May 2024, Narrows record). Pre-expansion Westridge handled only a few cargoes a month on the 300 kb/d system; that history is stated in the sheet, not keyed as flows.

Method: Facts compiled, expression ours (2026-10-17). Geometry backbone: the CER Pipeline Profile for the Trans Mountain Expanded System (Open Government Licence, Canada), which carries the Edmonton to Burnaby route, the Kamloops, Sumas and Burnaby delivery points and the U.S. section in northwest Washington; route corridor waypoints are real places on or near the published route through the Jasper corridor, the Coquihalla and the Fraser Valley. Every node is a real place at gazetteer coordinates; the Burnaby, Sumas and Westridge coordinates are the CER key-point coordinates in the held CER throughput file. Capacities and the committed split are from the house pipe profile (analysis/pipe_profiles/trans-mountain.md, sourced to Trans Mountain Q1 2025 disclosures and CER, September 3, 2025) and the house commitments grid (hidden_files/research/commitments-grid-partE.md, sourced to NEB RH-001-2012 and Trans Mountain disclosures). Flows are real CER monthly values from the house-held CER throughput file: system throughput is the CER system row (committed plus uncommitted volumes, converted at 6.28981 bbl per m3); Westridge flows are the CER Guide BB loadings series held in the Narrows record (narrows/data/westridge_monthly.json), which reconciles to the CER file Westridge key point within 0.1 kb/d of rounding; Sumas flows are the CER file Sumas key point. tolls.db was queried 2026-10-19 (python sqlite3): Trans Mountain is pipeline id 7, paths 34 (Westridge), 35 (Burnaby), 36 (Sumas) and 37 (Kamloops); the effective rows are the interim uncommitted tolls of Interim Tariff No. 123 (effective May 1, 2026). Toggle-ready fields follow builders/site/map_data/SCHEMA.md verbatim: facility_type on every node, product and system on every edge (system is trans_mountain, the balance model key, on every system edge; TMX is a label, never a key), capacity-state and toll fields only where a real source gives them; unknown stays absent, never interpolated.
