THE DURABLE NUMBERS

The succession
Federated Pipe Lines Ltd. (Imperial Oil / Home Oil - Anderson Exploration, 50/50) sold to Pembina Pipeline Corp. in 2000: C$340M including assumed debt, plus C$9M for the wholly-Imperial Cynthia Pipeline (121 km). The deal added about 3,200 km and took Pembina to about 7,000 km.
OGJ finance brief, August 1, 2000; OGJ, June 28, 2000; Pembina history page (via WS-H dossier 2D section A.2)

Swan Hills Pipeline - CONFIRMED
About 425 km, capacity 48,000 b/d, crude oil from the Swan Hills region to delivery points near Edmonton. The direct descendant of the 1959 Federated mainline (Swan Hills / Judy Creek / Virginia Hills to Edmonton).
Pembina AIF YE2023; Statistics Canada, 1961 (via WS-H dossier 2D sections A.1 and A.3)

Swan Hills deliveries
Edmonton only, for sweet crude: Enbridge Edmonton Terminal Yes, Trans Mountain Pipeline Yes, Strathcona Refinery Yes. No Hardisty leg and no Regina leg.
Pembina Swan Hills Toll Schedule, effective February 1, 2025 (via WS-H dossier 2D section A.3)

Swan Hills commercial form
Mainly interruptible (shippers transport mainly on an interruptible basis). Minimum tender 500 m3 per month; loss allowance 0.15%.
Pembina AIF YE2023; Pembina Swan Hills Toll Schedule (via WS-H dossier 2D)

Northern Pipeline - lineage INFERRED
About 700 km including laterals, NGL from Belloy, Alberta to Fort Saskatchewan. No Northern-only capacity is published (Peace plus Northern combined about 1.1 million b/d, 2024).
Pembina AIF YE2023 (via WS-H dossier 2D section A.3). System existence confirmed; Federated lineage inferred.

Northern inference basis
The Federated Pipe Lines (Northern) Ltd. line, Taylor, BC to Belloy, Alberta (172 km, 10-inch), was transferred to Pembina Northern LP in July 2000 (Certificate OC-42). Same origin, product family and destination as today's Northern system; Pembina publishes no mapping.
NEB Regulatory Agenda 2000-01, Files 3400-P123-2 and 3400-F72-1 (via WS-H dossier 2D section A.2)

Brazeau Pipeline - lineage INFERRED
About 500 km including laterals, capacity 61,000 b/d, NGL (ethane-plus) from gas plants southwest of Edmonton to Fort Saskatchewan. Receipts include Cavvy Caroline, Keyera Cynthia, Cenovus Sandcreek, Tidewater Acheson.
Pembina AIF YE2023; Pembina Brazeau Toll Schedule, June 2026 (via WS-H dossier 2D sections A.3 and B.4). System and capacity confirmed; Federated lineage inferred.

Brazeau inference basis
Federated's 1992 program expanded its main NGL pipeline, tied in the Caroline gas plant and paralleled Acheson to Fort Saskatchewan to move 30,000 b/d of NGLs from the Caroline plant - the same plant, product and destination as today's Brazeau trunk.
Home Oil Annual Report 1992 (via WS-H dossier 2D sections A.1 and A.3)

Western Pipeline - lineage INFERRED
About 400 km, crude from Taylor, BC to Prince George, BC. No capacity figure is disclosed in the sources consulted.
Pembina AIF YE2023 (via WS-H dossier 2D section A.3). System existence confirmed; Federated lineage inferred.

Western inference basis and open item
Federated (Western) held the Taylor to Kamloops line from 1994 and sold it to Pembina on July 31, 2000. Today's system ends at Prince George; when or why the Kamloops leg left the system was not found.
BC timeline submission to the NEB, July 10, 2006 (via WS-H dossier 2D sections A.2 and A.3, open item 5)

Confidence summary
Swan Hills: CONFIRMED (filed toll schedule plus AIF). Northern, Brazeau, Western: systems CONFIRMED to exist in the AIF, membership in the ex-Federated network INFERRED by geography and product - every such node and edge is labelled INFERRED and renders dashed.
WS-H dossier 2D section A.3 (ESTIMATE mapping) and open item 4

Tolls
Operator-posted Alberta schedules: Swan Hills effective February 1, 2025 (receipt tolls in C$/m3, for example Judy Creek Truck Terminal 17.79 and Cardinal battery 21.38 in the February 2023 schedule); Brazeau effective June 1, 2026 (ethane-plus receipts C$8.68 to 39.06/m3). No rows for any of the four systems in tolls.db - ingest backlog, not unpublished.
Pembina Swan Hills and Brazeau Toll Schedules; tolls.db query, 2026-10-19

Scale then and now
Federated transported about 65 million barrels in 1992 (about 178,000 b/d) across about 1,050 miles of pipe; its 1965 nameplate was 151,000 b/d. The surviving crude mainline, Swan Hills, is 48,000 b/d today - mature-field decline plus re-segmentation of legs into the other Pembina systems, with no published bridge.
Home Oil Annual Report 1992; Dept. of Energy, Mines and Resources table, 1967 vintage; Pembina AIF YE2023 (via WS-H dossier 2D section A.1)

NOTES

The Federated name is retired and was never Federated Co-operatives': Federated Pipe Lines Ltd. was an Imperial Oil / Home Oil (Anderson Exploration) company. Federated Co-operatives owns the Regina Co-op Refinery, which Federated never delivered to.

One geography, four systems: each system has its own line colour on the map and its own system tag on every edge, so the confirmed Swan Hills crude mainline reads separately from the three inferred-lineage systems.

Ethane-plus once flowed backwards on this geography: solvent for miscible floods moved from Fort Saskatchewan to the Swan Hills-area fields on the Federated system (about 1989). It is recorded here as history and not drawn; every arrow on the map is the current gathering direction.

The name Federated is retired. There is no Federated system, tariff or legal name in current Pembina disclosure: Pembina bought Federated Pipe Lines Ltd. in 2000 and re-segmented the network. Any note saying Federated is now called a single new name is wrong; the correct answer is four current names - Swan Hills, Northern, Brazeau and Western - on one geography (WS-H dossier 2D).

Federated Pipe Lines was never a Federated Co-operatives asset. It was owned 50/50 by Imperial Oil and Home Oil (later Anderson Exploration); Federated Co-operatives owns the Regina Co-op Refinery. The similar names are a conflation, and no leg to Regina is drawn (WS-H corrections register, item 1).

Confidence summary: Swan Hills is CONFIRMED - the filed toll schedule and the AIF document the system, its receipts and its Edmonton deliveries. Northern, Brazeau and Western are real, AIF-documented Pembina systems whose membership in the ex-Federated network is inferred from geography and product, because Pembina has never published the mapping; every such node and edge is INFERRED and renders dashed.

Direction history: crude and NGLs flowed field to Edmonton and Fort Saskatchewan, but ethane-plus solvent for miscible floods flowed the other way, from Fort Saskatchewan into the Swan Hills-area fields (about 1989, via WS-H section A.1). Arrows on this map show the current gathering direction only.

Not this map: Pembina's Nipisi and Mitsue pipelines are a separate heavy oil and diluent corridor from the Slave Lake country into Edmonton via the Swan Hills junction (Nipisi design 100,000 b/d). They are not Federated successor systems and are not drawn here (WS-H corrections register, item 6).

Also not this map: the Cynthia Pipeline bought with Federated in 2000 (121 km, C$9 million) survives as receipt names inside the Drayton Valley and Brazeau schedules (Bench Creek, GIOC and Base Element Cynthia terminals; Keyera Cynthia). Which current system holds the 2000 pipe itself is an open item (WS-H open item 4); the Cynthia node here stands for the documented Brazeau receipt, labelled INFERRED.

WITHHELD

The 1:1 successor mapping is withheld beyond Swan Hills: Pembina publishes no statement mapping Federated legs to today's Swan Hills, Northern, Brazeau and Western systems, and no statement of where the 2000 Cynthia Pipeline's pipe sits today (Drayton Valley, Brazeau, or both). The Swan Hills descent is documented (same geography, same filed system); the Northern, Brazeau and Western membership on this map is geography and product inference, labelled INFERRED on every such node and edge (WS-H dossier 2D section A.3 and open item 4).

Battery-level membership is not drawn and no battery is assigned to any of the four systems: WS-H Part 3 tested the held Petrinex data (June 2026) and found the battery-to-pipeline counterparty suppressed on 78.9% of Alberta battery oil-disposition volume, with no Alberta battery row naming a pipeline counterparty. Receipt points are shown at filed-schedule grain only.

Northern Pipeline capacity is absent: Pembina discloses Peace plus Northern combined (about 1.1 million b/d after Phase VIII, 2024; about 1.2 million b/d with the sanctioned Fox Creek to Namao expansion, in service Q1 2027), and no Northern-only figure exists in the sources consulted. The joint figure is never presented as a Northern figure, so no nameplate is carried on either Northern edge.

Western Pipeline capacity is absent: the AIF YE2023 description carried in WS-H gives length (about 400 km), product (crude) and endpoints (Taylor to Prince George) with no capacity figure, and no other source consulted discloses one.

Committed and uncommitted capacity are absent on every edge: Swan Hills service is mainly interruptible (Pembina AIF YE2023, carried as commitment_term on the trunk edge) and no committed split is published for any of the four systems, so committed_kbd and uncommitted_kbd stay absent throughout.

Monthly flow series are absent on every edge: no held monthly series exists for these systems, so flows_kbd is absent everywhere and nothing is interpolated. The historic Federated figures in WS-H (about 178,000 b/d transported in 1992; 205,400 b/d of gatherings in the nine months to September 1971) are pre-sale system history, printed in the print-sheet rows with their vintages, and are not carried as last_flow_kbd for any current system.

toll_refs are absent on every edge: tolls.db (queried 2026-10-19) carries no Swan Hills, Northern, Brazeau or Western pipeline, path or toll rows, so there is no tolls.id to cite. That is an ingest backlog item, never a market gap: the tolls are published in Pembina's own posted schedules (Swan Hills, effective February 1, 2025; Brazeau, effective June 1, 2026), cited in the trunk edges' toll_note. No Northern or Western toll schedule was extracted in the WS-H pass, so no toll_note is carried on those edges.

The Western endpoint change is not resolved and no Kamloops leg is drawn: Federated (Western) was Taylor to Kamloops in the 1994 to 2000 sources, while today's Pembina Western Pipeline is Taylor to Prince George (AIF YE2023). When or why the Kamloops leg left the system (sale, transfer or abandonment) was not found (WS-H open item 5).

The reverse solvent flow is not drawn: ethane-plus moved from Fort Saskatchewan to the Swan Hills-area fields for miscible floods on the Federated system in the 1980s (Alberta ethane policy implementation report, about 1989, via WS-H section A.1). It is history with the opposite direction to every live flow on this map, and is recorded in the notes rather than drawn as a route.
