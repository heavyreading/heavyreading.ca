Whale watch

Positioning in Western Canadian Select (WCS) futures: Commitments of Traders (COT) managed-money and commercial nets, how extreme current positioning is versus history, and the open-interest tripwire.

Big managed-money flows move WCS differentials before supply and demand have their say. This watch tracks the money in the ICE Canadian differential contracts - WCS 1A (Hardisty), WCS Houston, SW and C5 - in lots (1,000 bbl each): weekly Commitments of Traders (COT) positioning, probable whale flags, and a daily open-interest tripwire that fires before the Friday COT release. Brent and WTI positioning sit below as context; this page's edge is the Canadian barrels. When the detector sees a whale-sized build it raises a probable flag, with its confidence, its size, and what the differential did afterward. Probable, never binary: the page names the probable candidate - always as a probability, never as fact.

Walk-forward verdict, Sep 2026: no whale signal cleared the bar against the naive benchmark. This watch monitors for regime change.

How to read it: the snapshot is the latest Friday release (positions as of the preceding Tuesday; neither publisher revises). Rows tagged ICE come from the exchange's own weekly COT reports; the rest come from the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC). The flag feed is the detector's full history, newest first. Flags marked PUBLISHED DATA rest on published positioning data. Flags marked TEST DATA run on a placeholder feed while real history accumulates; treat them as illustrations, not real signals. The open-interest tripwire panel shows only contracts with a live OI feed.

How the flags are producedchanged Nov 18

First, the daily tripwire: the z-score of the daily change in open interest (OI) against its trailing 60-day history. A z-score of 3.0 or more fires; direction comes from five days of price action (rising OI with rising price reads as probable new longs, rising OI with falling price as probable new shorts, falling OI as liquidation). The OI feed is real ICE end-of-day data for the Canadian-differential contracts (history from 2026-09-21, accumulating) and the seeded stand-in elsewhere; every row says which. Second, the weekly attribution: the Friday Commitments of Traders (COT) release z-scores each leg's weekly change in net (managed money and commercial, i.e. producer/merchant) against that leg's own trailing 52-week history. The leg with the most unusual single-week move (|z| largest) owns the flag. |z| of 2.5 or more is whale-sized; 2.0 to 2.5 is the watch tier. There is no absolute lot floor: a whale in a small contract is a smaller whale than a whale in a big one, and the z-score is what makes that comparison honest. A single-week whale build reads as one probable player (high confidence). A multi-week build to the same size reads as accumulation (medium confidence). Watch-tier builds are sub-whale (low confidence, player unattributed). Positioning comes from the CFTC feed for WTI and the Canadian differentials and from ICE's own weekly COT PDFs for Brent and Gasoil. Every flag below names the probable candidate - always as probable, never as fact - and explains how it qualified. Flag colors follow the player type: amber for managed money (funds), green for commercial (producers).

What would make this page smarter: deeper history on the ICE end-of-day feed (the free Report Center window is 7 trading days, so the Canadian activity series starts 2026-09-21 and grows from there) and real settlement prices (flag backtests wait on them). Nothing on this page is a trading recommendation.
